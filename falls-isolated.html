Details

Simulation only. Each clip shows the complete twelve second trial, without a cut. Baseline and IMU supervisor trials begin with exactly the same joint state, tilt and angular velocity. The lowest collision surface starts 3 mm above the floor. There is no push or pose overwrite during the trial.

The stumble supervisor uses IMU tilt and angular velocity to stop the walking command while retaining the existing balance controller. Both versions recover this moderate stumble. Recovery here means no nonfoot floor contact, followed by two seconds of stable standing. It is not a get up from the ground.

The separate full fall candidate transitions into a fixed crouch and tuck after an IMU warning. It preserves joint gains and the original actuator torque limits. Forward head and torso floor contacts disappear in this fixture, but other body impacts remain. In the backward fixture, head contact falls while torso impact becomes much worse. Neither full fall recovers to stable standing. This controller is not safe for hardware.

Reported forces are sums of simulated floor contact normal forces on the named body region, measured after the rollout. They are not sensor inputs, injury estimates or calibrated physical measurements. Actuator torque limits do not bound passive impact forces.

All six prescribed initial conditions and all three controllers were evaluated, including mild, moderate and full forward/backward disturbances. The selected clips expose both the benefit and the failure. No learned fall policy was trained.
