# Post-miss stylized fall: rejected physical prototypes

This is a separate, hand-authored simulation experiment inspired by the end-pose/impact tradeoff in [Robot Crash Course](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10635v1). It does not reproduce Disney's learned policy, training scale, privileged critic or force-based training reward. The [author's publication page](https://www.baecher.info/publications/) and [official demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BXqpVMPk63A) provide primary context. Our contact forces are independent evaluation only; the controller accepts IMU, body encoders, RGB-D and known commands.

The historical 12-second receiver scene includes the original machine, receiving attempt, drop and natural fall. Its 3.45 m machine split is not the current two-yard formation or a qualified straight route. No push, initial tilt, mid-play root/joint overwrite, artificial support or pose animation is introduced. The evaluator's stop-on-fall is disabled only in the isolated driver so the complete landing remains visible. Original body gains and torque clamps are retained. This historical fixture has **no native actuator force limits**: the recorded `[0,0]` force ranges accompany `forcelimited=false`, and native finger force can exceed 170 N. It is not a hardware-qualified controller.

Three requested joint goals pass offline self-collision, mechanical-margin and head-clearance checks, but static stability and dynamic reachability are unverified. The requests are tuck, left-side roll and braced seated; none forces the simulator's root pose. Seated settling and autonomous standing recovery are different outcomes. The original receiving/fall controller and all earlier evidence remain unchanged.

The first matched pair uses a conservative 60 cm escaped-ball threshold. Its observer sees the approach but cannot confirm escape before visibility is lost; no intervention occurs. Both complete trajectories are exactly the fresh baseline. This negative is preserved in `receipt.json` and `linux/`.

One development correction on the known baseline freezes thresholds at 28 cm separation and 20 cm below the measured palm midpoint, with two fresh outward-moving frames and unchanged 6 cm confidence limit. This is posthoc development tuning, **not held-out validation**. Observed miss arming is 5.200 s and IMU confirmation 5.220 s, after the baseline's independently measured bilateral support ends at 4.823 s. All raw state/control/force arrays remain bit-exact before intervention. The fresh-frame uncertainty and margins are in the complete trigger log.

The corrected candidate is rejected. Head peak decreases 1182.8→489.6 N and maximum rolling 150 ms nonfoot normal impulse decreases 140.70→125.44 N·s, but total nonfoot peak increases 2755.2→6379.6 N. Weighted floor-normal squared cost increases 2.059e6→3.753e6 N²·s. It neither settles nor reaches a braced seated pose nor stands. A 0.282 rad mean end-joint error does not mean the free body attains the requested orientation; it ends lying and moving.

The commanded-target transition is also invalid: its maximum velocity is 3 rad/s, but acceleration including the first handoff is **576.50 rad/s²**, exceeding the declared 12. An earlier 138 rad/s² figure excluded that first transition and is superseded. The inherited gait command already has 14.53 rad/s velocity and 1514 rad/s² acceleration; a C2 handoff cannot instantly satisfy 3/12 bounds. Even braking 14.53 rad/s at 12 rad/s² needs 8.80 rad of joint travel. These are commanded-target derivatives, not measured motor-speed curves. No further late candidate was launched. A future experiment would need earlier sensor-armed gait conditioning and a newly matched baseline, potentially forfeiting the catch.

`independent-pair-audit.json` recomputes the prefix, impacts, complete command derivatives and actual joint-range excess from 1 kHz arrays. `protocol.json`, exact receipts and frozen `source-overlay-{r1,r2}.tar.gz` retain commands, source hashes, goals and failures. The overlays apply after the pinned base source/assets recorded in the receipt chain; they are not standalone hardware packages.

Both full-play films are in `media/{baseline,stylized-rejected}/full-play.mp4`: 301 unedited physical pose samples, 25 fps, 1280×720, 12.04 s. The matched presentation-camera rule shows machine, attempt, miss and complete failed fall. Source pose/time arrays are exact, MP4 metadata is checked and contact sheets were inspected. These are negative/blooper evidence, not graceful-fall or recovery demonstrations. Catch training continues separately and remains the priority.
